## 1 — Know what your MUSD is already committed to

Mezora: bills, cash commitments and payment receipts for small teams on Mezo. Show Workbench, app link and Mezo Testnet readiness.

## 2 — A wallet balance is only part of the picture

Bills due next week compete with today's spending. Recipient changes and duplicate invoices can lead to mistakes. A transaction hash still needs to be connected to the bill it paid. Target small teams and businesses that hold MUSD and pay contractors or suppliers. No adoption metrics claimed.

## 3 — From bill to committed cash to verified payment

Review bill → reserve MUSD → approve payment → reconcile receipt. Show a real bill and its receipt. Owner-cancelable reservations are budget commitments, not vendor escrow.

## 4 — Plan the business around its payments

Thirty-day cash plan, invoice checks, reviewed receivable allocation and statements. Documents and CSV imports create drafts. Recurring schedules generate drafts for review. Show cash planning and customer invoice screens.

## 5 — Shared approval verified on Mezo Testnet

Separate V2 contract enforces owner + reviewer quorum. Funding, reserve and payment of 1 MUSD are confirmed. Show completed V2 page and receipt. Main workspace remains V1, no migration occurred and V2 signers are immutable.

## 6 — Connect cash decisions to debt decisions

Read a Trove, preserve minimum debt and review partial repayment. A confirmed handoff links a 1-MUSD withdrawal with a separate 0.5-MUSD repayment. The unused 0.5 MUSD stays in the owner wallet. Show both receipts; do not claim atomic execution or trace specific fungible units.

## 7 — Working prototype and next steps

Hosted app, public source and testnet payment/repayment proofs. Deterministic proposals, owner signatures, no server custody. Next: user tests, general V2 integration and recovery design, then independent security review before a limited pilot. No mainnet release date or audit claimed. End with app/source URLs. Test transactions are self-funded, not revenue.
